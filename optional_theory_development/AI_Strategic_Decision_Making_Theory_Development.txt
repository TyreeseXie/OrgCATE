AI-ASSISTED STRATEGIC DECISION-MAKING
AND INNOVATION PRODUCTIVITY

AN ABDUCTIVE THEORY DEVELOPMENT BRIEF  |  ENGLISH EDITION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Population: U.S. publicly listed manufacturing and
            knowledge-intensive business service firms
Study window: 2018–2024    |    Treatment year: 2021


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
01  EMPIRICAL PHENOMENON
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Among U.S. publicly listed manufacturing and knowledge-intensive business
service firms observed during 2018–2024, the DML-estimated average treatment
effect of formally deploying AI-assisted strategic decision systems in 2021 on
innovation productivity in 2022–2024 is 0.9246 standardized units (95% CI:
[0.8210, 1.0283]).

A causal forest ranks pretreatment relative firm size (u) first among 12
candidate heterogeneity predictors (variable importance = 0.6573). A
training-only reconstruction, confirmed in held-out data, identifies an
inverted-U pattern: the estimated treatment effect peaks at u = 0.50 (1.9296)
and declines to approximately −0.1703 at u = 0.10 and u = 0.90.

These estimates establish a phenomenon to explain, not its organizational
cause. Variable importance does not identify a mechanism, and replication of a
fitted functional form does not establish why the pattern exists. The
theoretical puzzle is why medium-sized firms appear best positioned to turn
AI-assisted strategic decisions into innovation productivity.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
02  COMPETING ORGANIZATIONAL MECHANISMS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Two complementary—but empirically distinguishable—mechanisms are proposed:

    A   KNOWLEDGE RECOMBINATION CONVERTIBILITY
    B   ATTENTION–DECISION AUTHORITY ALIGNMENT

COMPARATIVE MECHANISM MATRIX
································
01  THEORETICAL LENS
    A   Knowledge-based view; organizational search; hybrid problem-solving
    B   Attention-based view; organization design; innovation selection

02  CORE CONSTRUCTS
    A   Knowledge coverage; interoperability; technical validation
    B   Managerial attention; authority clarity; resource-allocation friction

03  CAUSAL PATHWAY
    A   AI deployment → wider strategic search → knowledge recombination →
        viable innovations → higher productivity
    B   AI deployment → strategic recommendations → managerial attention →
        approval and execution → higher productivity

04  WHY RETURNS RISE AMONG SMALLER FIRMS
    A   Small firms initially lack expertise and validation capacity;
        increasing size makes AI insights more convertible
    B   Small firms initially lack specialized evaluation capabilities;
        increasing size strengthens informed adoption

05  WHY RETURNS FALL AMONG LARGER FIRMS
    A   Knowledge fragmentation, incompatible systems, and cross-unit
        integration costs undermine gains
    B   Agenda competition, approval delays, and resource-allocation conflicts
        hinder implementation

06  OBSERVABLE MEDIATORS
    A   Validated recombination rate; technical feasibility;
        knowledge-transfer costs
    B   Recommendation adoption rate; funding approval; decision latency; veto
        counts

07  BOUNDARY CONDITIONS
    A   Data interoperability; modular R&D architecture
    B   Decision authority clarity; cross-unit innovation governance

08  DISTINGUISHING PREDICTION
    A   Knowledge integration explains AI gains even after accounting for
        decision friction
    B   Decision friction explains AI gains even after accounting for
        knowledge quality

09  DATA REQUIREMENTS
    A   IT architecture; R&D knowledge structures; AI recommendation and
        project logs
    B   Organizational charts; decision records; budget approvals; employee
        surveys

10  KEY REFERENCES
    A   [1]–[3]
    B   [4]–[9]

The mechanisms can operate jointly, but they generate competing empirical
predictions. Mechanism A explains whether AI recommendations become
technically valuable innovation opportunities; Mechanism B explains whether
those opportunities receive attention, resources, and execution. Their
relative explanatory power may differ across firm-size ranges.

A relevant counterpoint is Babina et al. (2024) [10], who find that broader AI
investment can disproportionately stimulate product innovation in larger
firms. The present theory therefore concerns the incremental effect of
AI-assisted strategic decision systems on quality-adjusted innovation
productivity, not the universal impact of AI on innovation.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
03  TESTABLE THEORETICAL PROPOSITIONS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

P1  KNOWLEDGE CONVERTIBILITY AND THE ASCENDING SEGMENT
  Among firms with pretreatment size ranks u in [0.10, 0.50], the positive
  effect of 2021 AI deployment, relative to nondeployment, on 2022–2024
  innovation productivity increases with firm size. The mechanism is a larger
  stock of usable expertise and stronger cross-domain knowledge-recombination
  and validation capabilities.

P2  DECISION FRICTION AND THE DESCENDING SEGMENT
  Among firms with u in [0.50, 0.90], the effect of 2021 AI deployment,
  relative to nondeployment, on 2022–2024 innovation productivity weakens with
  increasing firm size and may become nonpositive at the upper end. The
  mechanism is growing managerial attention competition, hierarchical approval
  delays, and resource-allocation conflict.

P3  KNOWLEDGE INTEROPERABILITY AS A MODERATOR
  Among large firms with u in [0.75, 0.90], higher pretreatment knowledge
  interoperability makes the treatment effect of 2021 AI deployment on
  2022–2024 innovation productivity more favorable and attenuates the
  right-side decline. The mechanism is lower cross-unit knowledge-integration
  cost.

P4  DECISION AUTHORITY AS A MODERATOR
  Among large firms with u in [0.75, 0.90], clearer pretreatment professional
  decision authority and cross-unit budget coordination make the treatment
  effect of 2021 AI deployment on 2022–2024 innovation productivity more
  favorable and attenuate the right-side decline. The mechanism is more
  effective AI-recommendation adoption and execution.

All propositions concern conditional treatment effects (deployment versus
nondeployment), not the direct association between firm size and innovation.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
04  NEXT EVIDENCE
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4.1  TEST COMPETING MEDIATORS
  Measure M_A as the share of cross-domain AI recommendations technically
  validated and converted into R&D projects; measure M_B as the share of
  formally reviewed AI recommendations receiving committed implementation
  resources. Assess whether these pathways differentially explain
  treatment-effect heterogeneity. Where feasible, link 2021–2022 process
  measures to 2023–2024 outcomes and use longitudinal causal mediation methods
  with sensitivity analysis for posttreatment confounding.

4.2  INTRODUCE A NEW BOUNDARY CONDITION
  Measure pretreatment cross-unit task interdependence. Mechanism A predicts a
  stronger knowledge-integration bottleneck when interdependence is high.
  Mechanism B predicts that the productivity consequences hinge primarily on
  coordination structures and the alignment of expertise with decision
  authority.

4.3  COLLECT MULTILEVEL ORGANIZATIONAL EVIDENCE
  At the firm level, collect data standards, R&D knowledge dispersion,
  hierarchy, and approval rights. At the project level, collect AI
  recommendation quality, knowledge combinations, funding approvals, and cycle
  times. At the employee level, measure practices for questioning AI,
  perceived autonomy, cross-unit collaboration, and responsibility clarity. A
  2 × 2 business-unit field experiment crossing knowledge-integration support
  with decision-authority reform could distinguish independent and
  complementary effects.

4.4  IDENTIFICATION AND INTERPRETATION
  The fitted quadratic's symmetry around u = 0.50 is partly imposed by its
  functional form; it is not independent evidence of symmetric organizational
  mechanisms. The negative point estimates at the size extremes require local
  uncertainty estimates before being treated as statistically established.
  Follow-up work should allow asymmetric nonlinearities and test pretreatment
  trends, treatment overlap, patent-outcome timing, and mediator-outcome
  confounding.

CENTRAL THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTION
AI-assisted strategic decision-making may yield the greatest innovation
productivity gains when firms possess sufficient knowledge-conversion
capabilities without incurring excessive coordination and decision-execution
costs. Medium-sized firms may occupy this configuration more frequently, but
the proposed causal explanation rests on organizational mechanisms rather than
firm size itself.
